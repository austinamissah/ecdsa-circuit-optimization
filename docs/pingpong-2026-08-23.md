# The ping-pong construction, and two conclusions of mine that were wrong

**Everything else in `docs/` measures a circuit that no longer exists.** Between 2026-08-05 and
2026-08-23 the benchmark moved off the trailmix/dialog construction (1,154 qubits, score
1,486,468,554) onto a fixed-depth ping-pong division. The old code is still in the tree, gated
behind `SUB4_LEGACY_POINT_ADD`, and is not what ships. Upstream says the same about its own notes:
`src/point_add/memory/WAYFINDER.md` records that its notes 01 to 06 describe the old circuit and
their proven floors are not binding until re-derived. The documents in this directory are in
exactly that position, and they are kept as the record of what was measured, not as claims about
the circuit that ships today.

This file covers the new construction, what was built to measure it, and two published conclusions
that turned out to be wrong.

## What the construction is

A signed binary-GCD-style walk on a pair of registers, run at fixed depth. Each round records one
sign bit onto a tape; a replay pass consumes that tape once to build the coefficient; a reverse
walk restores the denominator and clears the tape. Both the divide and the multiply traversal use
the same machinery.

Two failure modes matter, and both are exactly checkable:

- **The walk.** Each round drops the top wire of both registers by XORing the bit below into it and
  freeing it, which is clean only if the value sign-extends into the round's scheduled width. The
  walk also has a fixed depth and must finish at (+/-1, +/-1). Running out of rounds is a failure,
  and at the shipped schedule it is the *only* walk failure observed: width violations do not
  occur at all.
- **The replay.** Its corrections are deliberately truncated to save gates, and the truncations
  fail at a measurable rate. Which truncation causes which kind of failure is not obvious and is
  set out below.

## The tooling: `tools/pp-screen/`

A classical pre-filter that reproduces the failure conditions in ordinary arithmetic, with no
quantum simulation, and stops at the first failing shot. Screening one nonce with `eval_circuit`
takes about 20 seconds; most nonces die here in milliseconds. See
[`../tools/pp-screen/README.md`](../tools/pp-screen/README.md) for usage and traps.

It is validated four ways, the last of which is the sharp one:

1. The shipped nonce, known clean, survives the full 9,024-shot screen with zero failures.
2. It independently reproduces upstream's measured λ_walk.
3. Its hand-rolled secp256k1 field and group are checked against the harness curve before any
   screening runs, and it aborts rather than warns on disagreement.
4. For a nonce the model rejects at shot X, the simulator's first failure Y must satisfy Y <= X,
   because a walk failure is a hard failure. Measured: for two nonces the model said 223 and 421
   and the simulator said 223 and 421. No false rejections in any run.

**The design decision worth carrying:** the width schedule and chunk geometry are *read from the
builder*, never recomputed. An env-gated dump (`PP_GEOMETRY`, applied by
`tools/pp-screen/instrument.py`) emits the resolved depths, the per-round widths and the truncation
windows. The schedule has moved three times and the chunk bounds are chosen per round against the
interleaving allowance. A screener that re-derives them does not fail loudly, it drifts, and drift
produces confident nonsense. This paid off within a day: after one sync the dump reported
`#rounds 696 697`, the two traversals having different depths, which nothing in the constants
predicts.

## Which truncation causes which failure channel

From reading the gates. It matters because it tells you what a classical filter can and cannot
screen for.

- **Chunk boundary carries and the overflow flag** are erased by `cmp_lt_phase_conditioned`,
  truncated to 22 bits. That correction is *phase*-conditioned: when the truncated comparison
  disagrees with the true carry, the shot takes a phase error, not a wrong value. These can never
  produce a classical mismatch.
- **The pseudo-Mersenne fold** adds one of `{-f, 0, +f, +2f}` into the low `REPLAY_FOLD_WINDOW`
  bits and discards the carry out of the top of that window. When that carry was needed, the value
  is wrong. This is the only replay channel that can produce a classical mismatch.

A useful correctness check if you implement the fold: in both the fused halving cell and the fused
doubling cell, the true carry out of fold position 0 works out algebraically to exactly the
quantity the circuit injects as `first_carry`. That identity is what makes the fold equivalent to a
plain windowed add.

## The search arithmetic, and the method error that produced a wrong one

Measured at `4eb93cb` with `lamscreen`, n=60 random nonces, full 9,024-shot screens:

| | |
|---|---|
| λ_classical | **17.717**, sd 3.608, sem 0.466 |
| 95% CI | 16.80 to 18.63 |
| var/mean | 0.735 |
| draws per clean nonce | 4.95 × 10⁷ (CI 1.98 × 10⁷ to 1.23 × 10⁸) |
| at 128 nonces/s | about 107 hours, CI 43 to 267 |

An earlier version of this estimate came from **n=8** with sd 6.0, so sem +/- 2.1, exponentiated
and reported as "2.9 × 10⁸ draws, 25 to 37 days, not viable on this hardware". The arithmetic was
fine. The problem is that on `e^λ` a +/- 2 error in λ is a 7× swing in the answer, so a point
estimate from a small sample is a guess with a decimal point on it. The corrected figure is 6 to 8×
smaller, and the wrong one had already caused a viable plan to be abandoned.

The rule: when the deliverable is `e^x` of a measured `x`, the confidence interval is the result,
not a footnote. Sixty draws cost about two minutes.

## Two levers that look large and are not

**The width schedule is a cliff, not a dial.** `SUB4_PP_SCHED_BIAS` narrows every scheduled width
by a constant, and the score column reads −0.37% per bit. Measured at `4eb93cb`, n=24 per arm:

| bias | score | λ_classical | range |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 1,163,865,710 | **17.0** | 10..23 |
| −1 | 1,159,533,691 (−0.37%) | **4,734.8** | 4,613..4,794 |
| −2 | 1,155,104,924 (−0.75%) | **8,976.9** | 8,964..8,997 |

One bit narrower fails 52.5% of all shots. The schedule already sits exactly on the deterministic
shrink envelope, so the first bit removed is the load-bearing one. Cost to establish: three
minutes.

**The qubit peak is not reachable with a knob.** `SUB4_PP_PEAK` sets the interleaving *budget*, not
the achieved width, and the budget already sits above what the circuit uses. Swept 1267 down to
1258 at `51c6c31`: the achieved peak stays 1267 at every setting and only the Toffoli count rises
(+784 at 1266, +10,368 at 1258). Gains on this axis have come from structural change, such as
making the interleaving plan direction-specific so the two traversals stop sharing one budget.

## Retracted: the square is not bounded

This is the second wrong conclusion, and it was refuted about twenty minutes after publication.

The square is isolable, which makes experiments here cheap:
`SUB4_PRODUCT_SQUARE_SELFTEST=1 ./target/release/build_circuit`.

Its structure is three half-width triangular squares, each computed into a product register,
accumulated into the output, then **fully uncomputed**: `row_addsub(inverse)` calls a real
`sub_full`, not a measured erasure. That makes roughly 83% of the square triangular row-adds paid
twice, and the uncompute is not removable, for the reason `03-proven-floors.md` gives in another
context: a compute/uncompute pair whose value is consumed in between is doing real work.

From that I priced Toom-3, the algorithm `WAYFINDER.md` names as the open lead, at five evaluations
of ~86 bits against three half-squares of 128: about 12,000 Toffoli gross, roughly 1.3% of the
circuit, before recombination that needs exact division by small constants, which in a reversible
circuit is multiplication by a modular inverse. That analysis still looks right.

Then I wrote that "the square is bounded, not open". That is a claim about the **surface**. What
was measured was one **algorithm**.

`c490604` added a second level of Karatsuba behind `SUB4_SQUARE_KARATSUBA2`. Measured here:

| | square executed Toffoli | square peak qubits |
|---|---|---|
| `SUB4_SQUARE_KARATSUBA2=0` | 58,709.125 | 1,273 |
| shipped, Karatsuba-2 on | **56,059.047** | **1,270** |

−2,650 executed Toffoli, 4.51% of the square and 0.292% of the circuit, plus three qubits off the
square's own peak.

The property used to dismiss Toom-3 was expensive recombination. Karatsuba-2 does not have it: its
recombination is adds and subtracts, no division by constants at all. The discriminating criterion
was in hand, applied to one candidate, and never turned around to ask which other candidate
satisfied it.

Worse, the structural model above *predicts* the win. Because the core is paid twice, anything that
shrinks it is worth roughly double its apparent saving, which argues **for** attacking the
algorithm. Right model, wrong implication read off it.

**A negative result about one method is not a negative result about the surface.** The heading is
what people remember, so the heading is what has to be true.

Still open: the core is now nine squares at 64 rather than three at 128, so the same question
applies one level down, where fixed per-branch recombination overhead starts to compete with a
shrinking core. Not measured.

## Traps found the hard way

- **A sync silently drops anything added under `src/point_add`.** The tree is taken from upstream
  wholesale, so instrumentation vanishes with no conflict and no warning. `instrument.py` re-applies
  it and `grind.sh` refuses to run without it.
- **The shipped configuration may not be in the file that defines it.** At `51c6c31`, `mod.rs` set
  the live values with `set_default_env`, and several differed from the literals in
  `pingpong_div.rs` (`R1` 318 against 340, `PEAK` 1267 against 1273, `ENDPOINT_FOLD_WINDOW` 30
  against 20). That block was deleted again at `d919bc6`. Confirm any baseline by setting the knobs
  explicitly to what you believe the defaults are and checking the build is identical.
- **`ops.bin` must be rebuilt for the exact configuration being screened.** The Fiat-Shamir seed
  hashes the whole op stream and the op count moves with the walk depth.
- **`eval_circuit` aborts before printing metrics on a dirty nonce**, so it cannot price a
  configuration that has not been ground yet. `PP_PROFILE=1` tracks the true 9,024-shot figure to
  within 0.005%, but samples 64 lanes, so its executed-Toffoli deltas carry roughly 85 counts of
  noise. Do not read a small difference off it as fact.
- **Line endings.** Several upstream files have been converted between LF and CRLF, which inflates
  diffs enormously: one submission reads as 11,165 changed lines in `mod.rs` and is 33 real ones.
  Check before concluding anything from a diff size.
- **Guards that hard-code "the default config" rot.** A driver here detected an ignored depth knob
  by comparing `ops.bin` against a hard-coded baseline; when the default changed it false-alarmed
  on a correct run and would have stayed silent on a colliding one. It now compares the depth the
  builder reports against the depth requested.
